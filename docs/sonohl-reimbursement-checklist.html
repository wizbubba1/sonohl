Reimbursement is three separate battles — coverage, coding, and payment — and none can start until FDA clearance exists. Work top to bottom. The amber gate below unlocks once you log the clearance letter and IFU.

Copy Website language: what to cut, what to use

FDA reads your website, brochures, and social posts as labeling. Pre-clearance, strip every term below.

diagnosticdiagnosesdetects disease accurateprovensafe and effective FDA-approvedFDA-clearedavailable now pricingsensitivity / specificity claims
Use instead
"SonoHL is an investigational multi-channel acoustic sensing platform in clinical development. It is not cleared or approved by the FDA and is not available for sale. Limited by Federal law to investigational use."

Post-clearance, mirror the cleared intended-use wording exactly and adopt SENSORA-style limiting language: support for the physician, not a sole means of diagnosis, significant only with physician over-read.

Watch Moving targets — monitor, don't depend on
  • TCET is paused for new candidates as CMS stands up the RAPID pathway.
  • RAPID (announced Apr 23, 2026) is new and largely unproven — promising for Breakthrough Devices, but unbattle-tested.
  • The alternative NTAP and OPPS pass-through breakthrough tracks are proposed for repeal effective FY2028 (FY2027 IPPS proposed rule).
  • The Health Tech Investment Act (S.1399) and the AMA "CMAA" concept are not law/policy — don't build a strategy that needs them.
Risk Gating risks to keep in view
  • No FDA clearance = no reimbursement, plus real promotional-liability exposure. This is the dominant present risk.
  • RPM/RTM eligibility hinges on FDA-defined medical-device status + automatic data capture — design and document to this now.
  • A Category III code carries no guaranteed payment; CMS's SaaS/AI methodology is unfinished.
  • Intended-use drift toward "diagnostic" or "autonomous" raises the evidence bar and creates misbranding risk. Stay on screening / decision-support, assistive-to-augmentative AI.

Informational, not legal advice. Engage FDA regulatory counsel and a reimbursement strategist before finalizing intended-use statements, website copy, or code applications.
